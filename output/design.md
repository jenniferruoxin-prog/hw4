# Campus Customs — Storefront Design (Problem 10)

Goal: make the site feel like a real Yale shop, not a demo, so shoppers trust it, stay longer and reach an in-stock item faster. Palette stays **black + Yale blue** (`#00366b`), with a small **gold** accent (`#f2c230`) for highlights. Styles live in `frontend/src/design.css` (loaded after `index.css`).

| # | Change | What I did | Why it helps customers stay and buy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Collegiate heading font** | **Playfair Display** (bold serif) for page, section, hero and product titles. **Graduate** (varsity block) for small labels like "OFFICIALLY LICENSED", "FIND YOUR FIT" and "QUARTER-ZIP PULLOVER". Section titles get a blue-and-gold varsity underline. Prices use lining numerals, so "$72.00" never reads like "$72.oo". | Feels like a university publication or athletics brand. The site instantly reads as Yale, and titles stand out. |
| 2 | **Clean body font** | **Inter** for body text, descriptions, prices, buttons, nav and chat, replacing Open Sans. Product names on cards stay in Inter bold for scanning. | Easy to read at small sizes and on phones, and it balances the expressive headings. |
| 3 | **Storefront hero** | Two columns: headline ("Wear your Bulldog pride, *from Old Campus to The Game.*"), a short value line, and two calls to action: **Shop the collection** and **Ask Handsome Dan**, which opens the chat. Three proof points: 102+ styles, Live size & stock, College, team & family gear. On the right: a collage of 3 real products as tilted polaroids, with name and price, that drop in. Faint diagonal stripes and a pennant stripe along the bottom. | Shows what we sell (real products, real prices) and the two main actions above the fold. |
| 4 | **Color swatches** | Round swatches instead of text chips. On product pages, the **garment color** is a large swatch with a check and an outline, labeled "Heather Gray / Shop more gray →". Print and logo colors are small dots. Cards show a row of color dots. Colors map from catalogue names to real shades. | Color is understood at a glance. The link turns "I like this color" into more items to browse. |
| 5 | **Handsome Dan the bulldog** | An SVG bulldog drawn in code (`Bulldog.tsx`): wide head, rose ears, jowls, underbite, and a Yale-blue collar with a gold **Y** tag. He's the **chat assistant**: the launcher button, a header avatar with an online dot, an avatar on every reply, "Sniffing through the stockroom…" while thinking, and a greeting in his voice. The agent prompt now speaks as Handsome Dan, with at most one light dog touch per reply. He also appears in the nav logo, the hero ("Need a size? Ask me — I check the stockroom live!"), the promo band, the footer and the favicon. | Yale's real mascot gives the shop a friendly face. A guide with a name invites more questions than a generic bot, and more questions mean more shoppers finding their size. |
| 6 | **Motion with purpose** *(extra)* | Hero text rises in and polaroids drop in. Cards fade up in a stagger. On hover, card photos zoom and show a "View details →" pill. The Dan launcher pops in, and a one-time "Need a size? **Ask Handsome Dan**" tooltip slides out. The chat panel and messages slide up. Dan bobs gently in the hero, and the logo wiggles on hover. All motion is disabled for shoppers who turn on **reduce motion** in their OS. | Motion shows what's clickable and draws the eye to the assistant and the products without getting in the way. |
| 7 | **Stock at a glance on every card** *(extra)* | Each card shows **size pills** (XS–XXL, with sold-out sizes greyed and struck through) and an honest stock **badge** from live inventory: "Every size in stock", "Few sizes left" (3 or fewer sizes) or "Low stock" (15 or fewer units). Name and price sit on one line. | Shoppers see whether their size is available **before** clicking, which means fewer dead ends. Real scarcity cues ("Few sizes left") encourage buying now without faking urgency. |

**Two deliberate changes from the brief (honesty):**
- There's no **"Design Your Own"** button, because the site has no customization feature. The second call to action opens the assistant instead.
- **Swatches don't switch the product preview.** Each catalogue product is one colorway with one photo, so letting shoppers "pick" another color would promise something that doesn't exist. Instead, the swatches show the item's real colors, and the garment swatch links to **more items in that color**, using the Problem 9 color filter.

**Checked in the running app (Edge, desktop 1400px and phone 390px):**
- All three fonts load.
- The badges shown come from live stock data.
- Clicking the gray swatch goes to `/products?color=Gray` (49 items).
- Dan answered "What does this one look like?" and "Is this in stock in medium?" on the Branford 1/4 Zip page correctly. For medium he said 20 available.
- On phones, the hero stacks, the tagline and tooltip hide so nothing overlaps, and the menu stays on the brand row.
